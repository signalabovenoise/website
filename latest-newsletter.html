Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • Indian stock markets closed lower on 22 September 2025, with the Nifty down by 124 points and the Sensex slipping 466 points, marking a second consecutive day of decline.
  • The decline was broad-based but prominently led by IT stocks, which were hit by negative sentiment following a sharp increase in the US H-1B visa fees.
  • Retail investor sentiment was cautious to negative, reflecting concerns over geopolitical and regulatory developments affecting key sectors.
  • Despite the losses, the overall market sentiment remains influenced by global central bank policies; earlier, US interest rate cuts had boosted confidence but recent developments overshadowed that.
  • The GIFT Nifty was marginally positive in early trading but eventually aligned with the broader market's downtrend by the close.

Sector Highlights

Commodities +0.69%

Commodities rose 0.69% supported by steady demand dynamics and supply considerations, while global commodity prices saw mild upticks influenced by currency movements as the US dollar weakened, aiding commodity exports and pricing power for Indian commodity companies.

Energy +0.69%

Energy sector gained 0.69% driven by positive sentiment around Adani Power, which hit a 15-month high following a stock split and extended gains for the second consecutive day. Strong buying interest and a notable 20% jump in Adani Power shares bolstered the sector's performance.

Media +0.48%

Media sector rose 0.48%, benefitting from increased investor interest in digital and cybersecurity firms as indicated by the premium listing of TechD Cybersecurity shares on NSE Emerge.

Pharma -1.41%

Pharma declined 1.41% due to sector-specific headwinds with no positive policy developments or earnings catalysts; pressure may be attributed to broader risk-off sentiment and possibly weakening outlook on pharma earnings or regulatory concerns affecting the sector.

IT -2.95%

IT sector plunged 2.95% primarily due to a large mutual fund sell-off triggered by uncertainty over US H-1B visa policy changes, which directly impacted investor sentiment on Indian IT stocks that rely heavily on US markets and offshore services.

Top News Developments
US H-1B Visa Fee Hike Hits Indian IT Stocks Hard
  • The US hiked H-1B visa fees to $100,000, increasing costs for Indian outsourcing companies and leading to a sharp sell-off in IT stocks.
  • Major players like Infosys, TCS, and Wipro saw significant losses, highlighting challenges in sustaining margins amid rising operational expenses.
  • The policy aims to reduce foreign talent dependency in the US, creating headwinds for Indian IT exports and outsourcing contracts.
FII Buying Resurgence Provides Market Stability
  • FIIs reversed their sell-off trend with three days of net buying last week, boosting liquidity and market confidence.
  • Improved macroeconomic conditions and currency stability are likely influencing FII behavior, supporting indices like Nifty and Sensex.
  • Sustained FII participation can cushion market volatility and attract domestic retail investors to equities.
GST Rate Cuts Boost Consumer Durables and Festive Demand
  • GST rate reductions on air conditioners and packaged water have sparked optimism in the consumer durables sector.
  • Companies like Voltas anticipate stronger sales during the festive season on improved affordability and robust consumer demand.
  • The policy shift is expected to drive growth in discretionary spending and benefit sectors tied to household goods and retail.

Emerging Opportunities

The domestic financial services sector shows promise due to consistent reforms and increasing financial inclusion initiatives driven by government policies.
