Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • The Indian stock market on 28 November 2025 showed a broadly rangebound performance with the Sensex marginally up by approximately 0.13% and the Nifty50 almost flat, reflecting cautious investor sentiment amid mixed domestic and global cues.
  • Market momentum was influenced by India's Q2 GDP growth previewed at an estimated 7.3%, supported by steady rural demand and government spending, although private capital expenditure remained subdued.
  • Despite record highs reached recently, the market faced downward pressure in the broader midcap and smallcap segments, which saw slight declines, and sector rotations influenced by profit concerns in technology stocks.
  • Retail investor sentiment appeared cautious yet optimistic, awaiting further clarity from October industrial and manufacturing data alongside upcoming policy developments.
  • Overall, the market narrative emphasized resilience with muted volatility, as participants balanced optimism from macroeconomic fundamentals against potential headwinds from global uncertainty and profit-taking activities.

Sector Highlights

Auto +0.62%

The auto sector gained 0.62% on November 28, 2025, driven by strong performance from major constituents like M&M and Adani Ports, which were among the top Nifty gainers. The sector benefited from positive sentiment around strong domestic demand and resilient quarterly growth expectations. Auto stocks have maintained momentum as part of the broader large-cap outperformance this month, with the sector being one of the standout performers since the market's previous all-time high in September 2024.

Pharma +0.59%

Pharma stocks rose 0.62% on November 28, with Sun Pharma among the top Nifty gainers. The sector's strength was bolstered by Sudeep Pharma's strong market debut, closing 30% higher at Rs 773.70 after listing at a 23% premium to its IPO price, reflecting strong investor confidence in pharmaceutical equities. The sector has been a consistent performer this month and continues to benefit from stable domestic demand dynamics.

Media +0.55%

Media stocks gained 0.55% on November 28, contributing to the day's modest upside. The sector has shown resilience as part of the broader market rally, though it experienced some volatility. Media stocks rallied to fresh record highs on November 27 before profit-taking emerged, and the sector continues to track overall market sentiment driven by expectations of interest rate cuts from both the Federal Reserve and Reserve Bank of India.

CPSE -0.59%

CPSE stocks declined 0.59% on November 28 as part of broader selling pressure in power and infrastructure sectors. Power Grid Corporation was among the top losers on the day. The sector has been the biggest underperformer since the market's previous all-time high 14 months ago, with the BSE Utilities index down 23%, driven by weaker-than-expected power demand and operational inefficiencies affecting major state-owned enterprises.

PSE -0.65%

PSE stocks fell 0.65% on November 28, with power and oil & gas sectors shedding 0.5-1% each. The decline reflects ongoing structural headwinds facing public sector enterprises, particularly in utilities and energy. The power sector has struggled due to weak demand, while the oil & gas sector faced uncertainties from geopolitical conflicts affecting crude prices. These sectors represent the weakest performers since the market's previous record high in September 2024.

Top News Developments
FIIs Pull Out ₹1,255 Crore on Budget Jitters
  • Foreign investors turned net sellers due to apprehension over new tax provisions in the Union Budget and weakening global sentiment.
  • This outflow puts pressure on the Indian Rupee, which is already trading near its record low, prompting potential RBI intervention.
  • The selling was concentrated in cyclical sectors like banking and IT, while defensive sectors saw relatively less impact.
  • While DIIs are currently absorbing the selling pressure, sustained FII outflows could trigger a broader market correction in the coming weeks.
India's Q2 GDP Growth Surges to 8.2%, Beating Forecasts
  • The stronger-than-expected GDP growth provides a significant positive macroeconomic backdrop, suggesting underlying economic resilience.
  • This robust data could attract foreign investment once current budget-related concerns subside, providing a long-term tailwind for the market.
  • Sectors tied to domestic consumption and manufacturing are likely to benefit most from this strong economic performance.
  • The high growth figure may give the RBI more room to focus on inflation control without worrying about stifling economic activity.
Adani Defence Expands into Aviation Training with ₹820 Crore Buyout
  • Adani Defence acquired a 72.8% stake in FSTC, a pilot training firm, signaling an aggressive push into the defence and aerospace ecosystem.
  • This move leverages the government's focus on indigenous defence manufacturing and the rising demand for pilots from expanding Indian airlines.
  • The acquisition creates synergies with Adani's existing airport operations, potentially allowing them to offer bundled services to airlines.
  • For investors, this deal solidifies the Adani Group's long-term strategy of building a comprehensive infrastructure portfolio, though the capital outflow is a near-term factor to watch.
Bandhan Bank Initiates Major Balance Sheet Cleanup
  • The bank's board approved the sale of its Non-Performing Asset (NPA) and written-off loan portfolios worth over ₹6,900 crore.
  • This strategic move is aimed at cleaning up its balance sheet, improving asset quality, and freeing up capital for future lending growth.
  • While the sale will likely lead to a one-time hit on profitability in the near term, it is a long-term positive for the bank's financial health and stability.
  • This action could set a precedent for other banks struggling with asset quality, reinforcing a positive trend of balance sheet strengthening across the Indian banking sector.

Emerging Opportunities

Renewable energy continues to emerge as a promising sector supported by recent policy incentives and India’s push towards sustainable infrastructure investments.
Financial technology companies are gaining traction amid digital payment innovations and expanding financial inclusion, presenting medium- to long-term growth potential.
Pharmaceuticals and healthcare remain attractive owing to ongoing innovation, export potential, and increased domestic demand post-pandemic.
Infrastructure development, boosted by government spending in transportation and urban development projects, offers significant growth opportunities.
Contrarian opportunities exist in select midcap and smallcap stocks that have been temporarily undervalued due to short-term profit concerns but possess strong fundamentals aligned with India’s long-term economic growth story.

Policy & Regulations

  • Market regulator SEBI has introduced a new incentive structure for mutual fund distributors to deepen market participation across the country.
  • This initiative specifically aims to encourage bringing in new investors from beyond the top 30 cities (B30) and to increase the number of women investors, potentially boosting long-term retail inflows.
  • In a separate development, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) announced a reduction in the futures and options (F&O) lot size for key indices, which could lower the capital required for retail traders to participate in derivatives.
