Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • Indian equity benchmarks ended almost flat on November 13, 2025, with the Sensex and Nifty closing marginally higher after a volatile session marked by sectoral swings and muted investor sentiment.
  • Retail investor sentiment remained cautious as the market digested the sharp drop in India's retail inflation to 0.25% in October, which broadly matched expectations and contributed to a muted start.
  • Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) continued their buying streak, marking their 14th consecutive session of net inflows, while foreign institutional investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, reflecting a divergence in investor appetite.
  • Market volatility eased slightly, with the India VIX index declining by 3%, indicating reduced uncertainty and a more stable trading environment.
  • Overall, the market extended its recent gains, supported by optimism around US-India trade talks and hopes for a resolution to the US government shutdown, but gains were limited by profit booking and mixed global cues.

Top News Developments
Tata Steel's Profit Skyrockets on Strong Domestic Demand
  • The company reported a massive 4.2x jump in Q2 net profit to ₹3,183 crore, driven by strong domestic sales and better operational efficiency.
  • This performance highlights the positive impact of India's infrastructure and manufacturing push, which is creating sustained demand for steel.
  • The strong earnings have improved investor sentiment for the entire metals and mining sector, signaling a potential bullish trend for related stocks.
  • For retail investors, this points to improved earnings visibility and a potentially strong medium-term outlook for the steel industry.
Government Rolls Out ₹25,060 Crore Export Boost for MSMEs
  • The cabinet approved a major financial stimulus to enhance India's export competitiveness, directly benefiting manufacturing and MSME sectors.
  • By expanding credit guarantee schemes, the policy aims to lower financing costs and improve liquidity for exporters, a key growth engine.
  • This move is expected to drive capital investments in export-heavy industries like textiles, chemicals, and engineering goods.
  • Investors should watch for positive impacts on companies within these export-oriented sectors and the banks that finance them.
NIIF Plans Stake Sale in EV Maker Ather Energy
  • The National Investment and Infrastructure Fund's plan to sell a ₹551 crore stake in Ather Energy signals institutional profit-taking in a high-growth sector.
  • The deal's floor price of ₹620 per share sets a near-term valuation benchmark for the unlisted EV company and its peers.
  • While such a sale could cause short-term price volatility in the EV space, it also signifies the sector's growing maturity and can attract new strategic investors.
  • This development is a key sentiment indicator for the entire electric vehicle ecosystem, including listed auto ancillary suppliers and competitors.

Emerging Opportunities

The sharp decline in retail inflation to 0.25% in October signals potential for monetary policy easing, making interest-rate-sensitive sectors like real estate and consumer finance attractive for medium- to long-term investment.
Progress in US-India trade talks opens up opportunities in export-oriented sectors and companies with strong international linkages.
The government's approval of a ₹25,060-crore export promotion mission and expansion of the credit guarantee scheme could benefit MSMEs and export-focused industries.
Continued net buying by domestic institutional investors suggests underlying strength in the domestic market, favoring large-cap and blue-chip stocks with stable fundamentals.
The easing of market volatility, as reflected in the decline of the India VIX, presents a window for contrarian investors to accumulate quality stocks at relatively stable valuations.

Policy & Regulations

  • The Union Cabinet has approved a significant ₹25,060 crore export promotion mission aimed at enhancing India's global trade competitiveness.
  • As part of this initiative, the government also expanded the credit guarantee schemes available to exporters and Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), which is expected to lower their financing costs.
  • This policy is a direct stimulus for export-oriented sectors such as textiles, chemicals, and engineering goods, and is likely to improve liquidity and encourage capital investment within these industries over the medium term.
