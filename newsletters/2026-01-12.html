Top News Developments
Persistent FII Outflows & Global Cues Drag Indian Market
  • Indian benchmarks opened lower due to continued FII net selling of ₹3,769 crore on January 9 and volatile crude prices, signalling a cautious sentiment in the market that contributed to a five-session losing streak before a modest rebound.
  • FIIs have maintained a net selling stance for several sessions, including a significant monthly net outflow of ₹34,349 crore in December 2025, which reduces market liquidity and caps upside potential, disproportionately impacting mid and small-cap segments.
  • Global cues, encompassing US inflation data, AI-driven chip earnings, and ongoing geopolitical tensions, are intensifying investor uncertainty, making the Indian market susceptible to broader international shifts and adding to the volatility.
  • The rupee depreciated five paise against the US dollar, exacerbated by rising crude oil prices and continued foreign fund outflows, which could lead to increased import costs and inflationary pressures for the Indian economy.
  • Despite robust DII buying of ₹5,596 crore on January 9 providing some market support, investors are advised to be selective and focus on large-cap stocks until the Union Budget, as markets navigate a consolidation phase amidst these global risks and unstable domestic fundamentals.
Defence & Infra Stocks Face Headwinds Amid Technical Weakness
  • Defence and infrastructure companies like Tejas Networks (-10.98%), GE T&D India (-5.60%), Reliance Infra (-5.00%), Elecon Engineering (-4.75%), and BEL (-1.43%) saw sharp declines, driven by broader bearish technicals and a lack of specific positive news, signalling a sector rotation away from high-beta plays.
  • Reports of India easing restrictions on Chinese companies bidding for government contracts immediately intensified competition concerns, leading to significant declines in shares of domestic players such as BHEL, GE Vernova T&D India, and Cummins India, highlighting policy-driven market sensitivity.
  • This underperformance aligns with broader market sentiment shifting away from high-beta plays amidst FII outflows and oil volatility, which can negatively impact capital expenditure projects and potentially constrain supply chains in these critical sectors.
  • While no explicit news triggers were reported for all individual declines, the overall market technicals, including the Nifty trading below key moving averages and a negative MACD, contributed to the sector-specific weakness and investor exit.
  • Investors should closely monitor future policy developments regarding foreign competition and sector-specific deal flows, as sustained negative sentiment could delay infra policy momentum and impact the long-term earnings outlook for these defence and infrastructure companies.
IT Sector Cautious Ahead of Q3 Earnings Amid Global Headwinds
  • Shares of IT majors like TCS and HCL Technologies traded in the red at open, reflecting pre-earnings caution among investors keenly awaiting their Q3 results, following Avenue Supermarts' mixed reaction to its earnings.
  • TCS, despite reporting a 14% YoY decline in consolidated net profit, showed nearly 5% YoY revenue growth in Q3FY26 and declared a significant dividend of ₹57 per share, indicating a mixed but resilient performance amidst challenging global conditions.
  • Investor focus for IT companies is now shifting beyond just quarterly numbers to management outlook, the impact of AI on future growth, and margin resilience, especially given a generally modest December quarter for the sector.
  • Weak global cues, particularly potential US slowdown risks and continued FII short positions, are collectively weighing on export-heavy IT companies, tempering expectations despite ongoing deal wins and long-term digital transformation trends.
  • The IT sector, especially export-oriented firms with significant US exposure, is under close watch; while short-term sentiment might be volatile due to individual results, strong guidance focusing on AI adoption and strategic deals could still present bullish opportunities in subsequent quarters.

Policy & Regulations

  • India's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND) has intensified its regulatory scrutiny by issuing formal notices to 25 cryptocurrency exchanges, including BingX and LBank, for failing to register and comply with anti-money laundering (AML) requirements, signaling a significant tightening of the regulatory environment for digital asset platforms.
  • The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is planning to simplify trading rules, increase regulatory responsibility for stock exchanges, and potentially overhaul broker net-worth norms and surveillance, aiming to foster a more robust and compliant market ecosystem.
  • Reports indicate that India is easing restrictions on Chinese companies bidding for government contracts, a policy shift that has already caused sharp declines in shares of domestic players like BHEL, GE Vernova T&D India, and Cummins India due to heightened competition concerns.
  • In a potentially positive development for domestic solar firms, China's move to cancel or cut export tax rebates on solar and battery products could ease global pricing pressures, thereby benefiting Indian companies such as Waaree Energies and Premier Energies.
