Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • On 3 December 2025, the Indian stock market showed mild negative sentiment with the Nifty 50 consolidating below the crucial 26,000 level and Sensex closing slightly down by about 31 points, reflecting short-term caution and lack of follow-through buying.
  • The market faced selling pressure for the third/fourth consecutive session, mainly due to persistent foreign institutional investor (FII) selling, although domestic institutional investors (DIIs) continued to absorb some selling by net buying.
  • Profit-booking in key private banks and financial sectors weighed on overall market indices, despite strong underlying GDP growth and expectations of a rate cut by the Reserve Bank of India later in December.
  • Broader market segments like midcaps and smallcaps experienced underperformance compared to benchmarks, with sectoral indices showing mixed performance—financials and banking pressured, while pharma showed defensive strength.
  • The Indian Rupee hitting a record low near ₹90 against the US dollar also contributed to risk-off sentiment, though early indications from GIFT Nifty around 26,200 suggested a potential gap-up start to the trading day driven by value buying and positive global cues.

Sector Highlights

IT +0.76%

The IT sector gained +0.76% driven by rupee depreciation to a fresh record low of ₹90.25 per USD. With the majority of IT sector revenues denominated in US dollars, the weakened rupee enhances earnings conversion and margins. Major gainers included Wipro (+2%), TCS (+2%), Infosys (+1%), Mphasis (+1%), and Tech Mahindra (+1%), reflecting strong investor optimism in the technology space. The Nifty IT index climbed to become one of the top sectoral gainers, with the rupee weakness stemming from persistent foreign portfolio outflows and stalled India-US trade talks.

Media +0.25%

The Media sector posted a modest gain of +0.25%, though no specific drivers or news items were identified in available market data for this sector on December 3, 2025.

Bank +0.13%

The Banking sector showed minimal movement with a +0.13% gain. However, the broader financial sector faced headwinds as the RBI Monetary Policy Committee meeting commenced on December 3, creating a cautious market sentiment. Investors adopted a wait-and-watch approach ahead of central bank policy cues on inflation, growth outlook, and interest rates, which limited upside momentum in banking stocks.

CPSE -1.22%

The CPSE sector declined -1.22% amid broader market weakness and investor caution ahead of the RBI MPC meeting. The cautious sentiment following the previous session's weak close (Sensex down 0.59%, Nifty50 down 0.55%) extended into CPSE performance, with limited sector-specific positive catalysts to offset the broader market headwinds.

PSU Bank -3.07%

The PSU Bank sector experienced a sharper decline of -3.07%, reflecting heightened caution in the banking space ahead of the RBI Monetary Policy Committee announcement. The wait-and-watch approach by investors regarding interest rate decisions and inflation outlook particularly impacted PSU banking stocks, which are sensitive to policy shifts. Additionally, the broader market weakness and previous session's decline carried over into this session.

Top News Developments
RBI Policy Meeting Creates Market Jitters
  • The market is in a cautious wait-and-watch mode as the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meeting begins, leading to profit booking and a fourth straight session of losses for benchmark indices.
  • Investors are divided on whether the RBI will cut the repo rate by 25 basis points, as robust GDP growth of 8.2% complicates the decision despite record-low inflation.
  • A rate cut would be a major positive for interest-rate sensitive sectors like banking, real estate, and autos by reducing borrowing costs and boosting credit demand.
  • The RBI's decision will also be influenced by global trends, where major central banks like the US Federal Reserve are now expected to start cutting rates, creating room for a more accommodative stance.
Rupee Plunges to Record Low Below 90/USD
  • The Indian Rupee breaching the psychological 90-per-dollar mark is a major headwind for the market, driving sustained FII outflows and increasing import costs.
  • This depreciation directly benefits export-oriented sectors like IT and Pharma, whose earnings in dollar terms translate to more rupees, leading to outperformance in their respective indices.
  • Conversely, sectors reliant on imports, such as oil & gas, auto components, and electronics, will face significant margin pressure due to higher input costs.
  • The currency's weakness has also fueled a rally in commodities like gold and silver, which have hit record highs as investors seek safe havens against currency devaluation.
IPO Market Booms Amid Secondary Market Caution
  • E-commerce platform Meesho's ability to raise ₹2,439 crore from anchor investors ahead of its IPO highlights strong institutional demand for India's digital growth story.
  • This trend reveals a broader shift in FII strategy, where foreign investors are selling holdings in the secondary market but are eager to invest in promising companies through the primary market.
  • The success of recent IPOs like Aequs, which was oversubscribed on its first day, indicates robust retail appetite for new listings with strong fundamentals.
  • This IPO frenzy intensifies competition in sectors like e-commerce and digital retail, putting pressure on established players like Reliance Retail to innovate and retain market share.
Promoter Stake Sales Signal Shifting Tides
  • A significant trend of promoter selling has emerged, with owners of major companies like IndiGo, Vedanta, and Whirlpool collectively selling stakes worth over ₹12,000 crore.
  • This activity could suggest that promoters believe their company valuations are at or near a peak, choosing to cash out or diversify their personal wealth.
  • For retail investors, heavy promoter selling can be a red flag, potentially indicating a lack of confidence in the company's near-term growth prospects.
  • This trend stands in contrast to the strong buying from DIIs, creating a dynamic where domestic funds are absorbing shares being offloaded by both FIIs and company insiders.

Emerging Opportunities

The financial services sector, despite near-term weakness, offers medium- to long-term opportunities owing to upcoming RBI rate cuts expected to spur lending growth and credit demand.
Defensive sectors like pharmaceuticals are attracting investor interest due to their resilience and stable earnings profile amid broader market volatility.
Technology-enabled companies, including those involved in AI acquisitions (e.g., Tata Communications’ recent acquisition of an AI firm stake), present promising growth prospects leveraging digital transformation.
The metal sector may benefit from global commodity price stabilization and China's stimulus efforts, making selective metal stocks attractive for cyclical recovery plays.
Contrarian investments in large-cap stocks experiencing profit-booking or foreign outflows but supported by strong domestic demand and institutional buying, such as some private banks and consumer goods companies, could yield medium-term gains.

Policy & Regulations

  • SEBI is reportedly considering new eligibility criteria for derivatives trading, potentially linking access to the Futures and Options (F&O) segment to an investor's existing equity portfolio size.
  • This move could significantly impact retail participation in the F&O market by introducing stricter entry barriers, aiming to curb speculative activity.
  • In a separate development, the government has decided against increasing the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) limit in public sector banks, which led to a sharp fall in the Nifty PSU Bank index as it dampened investor hopes for capital infusion and reforms.
  • The RBI also reaffirmed the status of SBI, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank as Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs), mandating them to maintain higher capital buffers to ensure financial stability, which reinforces their systemic importance but also increases their capital requirements.
