Fundamentals Corner
Despite strong historical ROE and ROCE, this stock shows significant deceleration in sales and profit growth, with recent quarterly net profit even declining.
While past fundamentals were robust, the current P/E of 23.7 appears elevated against a backdrop of weakening profitability and a negative stock price trend over the past year. Investors should exercise caution and seek signs of profit growth re-acceleration.

  • As one of the biggest gainers among the top-10 most valued firms last week, Infosys contributed substantially to the market's positive momentum, underscoring its pivotal role in driving sectoral growth.

Top News Developments
Infosys Q3 Beat Ignites IT Rally, Signaling Sector Rebound
  • Infosys's strong Q3 FY26 results, surpassing expectations, provided a much-needed boost to the Nifty IT index, indicating a potential turnaround in client spending and overall IT demand, countering earlier weak broader earnings sentiment.
  • This performance, alongside strong showings from HCL Tech and modest growth from TCS, suggests that the worst may be over for the Indian IT services sector, leading to increased FII interest and potential liquidity for IT stocks.
  • While Wipro saw a profit decline, the overall positive trend for key players like Infosys and Netweb Technologies (record Q3, AI-driven revenue) highlights a measured recovery focused on AI integration and improving deal quality.
  • The rally in IT heavyweights like Infosys, a top-10 gainer, could inspire spillover effects into mid-cap IT stocks and provide broader market support, despite ongoing FII outflows from other sectors.
India-US Trade Deal Nears Finish Line, Boosting Export Sectors
  • The Commerce Secretary's announcement regarding the nearing finalization of the first tranche of the India-US trade deal injects positive sentiment into the market, easing trade uncertainties and aligning with India's manufacturing and PLI initiatives.
  • This development is crucial for sectors like manufacturing, exports, and pharmaceuticals, as it promises enhanced export liquidity and reduced policy risks, potentially attracting foreign institutional investment back into these segments.
  • Such a deal could help temper persistent FII selling pressure seen in January by improving India's global trade outlook and strengthening investor confidence in the country's economic partnerships.
  • Geopolitical tensions, like US President Trump's new tariff threats related to Greenland, while causing global market unsettlement, might also open up longer-term trade negotiation opportunities for India, complementing the US trade deal efforts.
FIIs Continue Selling Spree, DIIs Provide Cushion Amid Market Volatility
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have maintained a significant selling streak in January, withdrawing over ₹22,500 crore from Indian equities, driven by rising US bond yields, a stronger dollar, and concerns about India's AI-led growth relative to peers.
  • This persistent selling tightens market liquidity and has particularly impacted large-cap sectors, contributing to Nifty's year-to-date decline and intensifying volatility, especially with critical Q3 results from banking heavyweights like ICICI Bank and HDFC Bank due.
  • However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) have acted as a crucial counterweight, providing substantial support by buying ₹34,076 crore in equities during the same period, largely offsetting FII outflows and preventing a steeper market correction.
  • Heightened volatility is anticipated in the January series due to FII short positions in index futures, alongside geopolitical tensions between the US and Iran and US President Trump's tariff threats, demanding a cautious approach from retail investors.
  • Despite the FII outflows, analysts still project a 'Goldilocks' recovery for Indian markets in 2026, driven by strong domestic earnings growth and policy support, suggesting that the current pressure might be a temporary phase.

Emerging Opportunities

IT sector outperformance presents a contrarian opportunity; IT stocks gained 2.9% with key gainers including Infosys (5.58%), Tech Mahindra (5.26%), and HCL Tech (1.67%), suggesting relative strength despite broader market weakness.
Banking sector quarterly results from ICICI Bank and HDFC Bank, along with lenders like IDBI Bank, RBL Bank, and YES Bank, represent key catalysts; Bank Nifty continues to outperform with potential to push toward 61,200-62,000 if it closes above 60,500.
Midcap and smallcap segments show selective resilience with Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 rising 0.20% and 0.46% respectively, indicating buying opportunities beyond large caps amid broad market weakness.
The Union Budget 2026 (February 1) presents a major catalyst for policy-driven sectoral opportunities; analysts expect meaningful trends to emerge post-Budget across sectors aligned with growth support and fiscal responsibility priorities.
Contrarian positioning opportunity exists as the market has potentially factored in current negative catalysts; domestic institutional investors (DIIs) have purchased ₹37,000 crores in the past month while FIIs sold ₹41,000 crores, suggesting potential mean reversion as FII selling pressures eventually ease.

Policy & Regulations

  • The Commerce Secretary's announcement that the first tranche of an India-US trade deal is nearing finalization provides a strong positive signal for Indian markets, promising enhanced export opportunities for sectors like manufacturing, pharma, and IT. This strategic development could help mitigate FII selling pressure by reducing policy uncertainty and making India a more attractive destination for foreign capital.
  • Market participants are actively pushing the government for capital market tax relief in the upcoming budget, specifically advocating for a higher exemption limit on long-term capital gains, which would significantly benefit retail investors. Such a policy change could incentivize greater retail participation and long-term equity investment by increasing net returns and improving investor confidence in the market.
  • The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has imposed a substantial penalty of ₹22.20 crore on InterGlobe Aviation (IndiGo) for mass flight cancellations and operational disruptions. This regulatory action highlights increased scrutiny on airline operational efficiency and consumer protection, implying potential compliance costs and reputational risks for the airline sector while also serving as a deterrent against similar service failures.
