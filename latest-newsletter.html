Sector Highlights

CPSE +0.12%

CPSE gained +0.12% on February 12, 2026. Nifty PSU indices advanced around 1% on Wednesday, reflecting selective strength in public sector enterprises. PSU banking stocks continued to outperform their private sector counterparts, providing support to the broader CPSE sector.

Metal +0.02%

Metal sector showed minimal movement at +0.02% on February 12, 2026. While no sector-specific drivers were identified in available market reports, the sector remained largely range-bound amid broader market consolidation and mixed global cues affecting commodities and capital-intensive industries.

Bank -0.01%

Bank sector declined marginally at -0.01% on February 12, 2026. Nifty Private Bank slipped 0.2% as broader market conditions remained subdued. However, Bank Nifty formed a bullish hammer-like candle with a long lower shadow, signaling buying interest at lower levels and suggesting potential recovery if the index moves above Monday's high of 60,876.

Realty -1.45%

Realty sector declined 1.45% on February 12, 2026. While specific realty-focused triggers were not detailed in market reports, the sector was caught in broader profit-booking and cautious sentiment as investors awaited Q3 earnings results and monitored macro developments including US employment data and Federal Reserve rate cut expectations.

IT -5.51%

IT sector fell 5.51% on February 12, 2026, with Nifty IT declining 1.8% and the sector slumping 12% for the year. The sharp decline was driven by the 'Anthropic shock'—concerns about AI disruption in the technology sector—which triggered a significant selloff in US-listed ADRs of top Indian IT companies on the previous trading day, spilling over into domestic markets. Tech stocks were expected to struggle in the near term as investors rotated away from IT toward better-performing sectors.

Top News Developments
Structural Derating Hits Indian IT Sector
  • The Nifty IT index plunged over 4% as agentic AI advancements from competitors like Anthropic threaten the traditional labor-arbitrage billing models of Indian firms.
  • TCS losing its status as India’s second-most valuable company to ICICI Bank underscores a fundamental shift in investor preference toward domestic credit growth.
  • Moody’s estimates that potential US H-1B visa fee hikes could cost Indian IT majors $250 million, further squeezing margins already strained by AI R&D costs.
  • Global 'super-investor' activity suggests that while service providers face disruption, platform owners with integrated AI are the primary beneficiaries of the current tech cycle.
US Macro Data Dampens Rate Cut Expectations
  • Stronger-than-expected US January jobs data has reduced Federal Reserve rate-cut odds, leading to higher US Treasury yields near 4.18% that act as a gravitational pull on global equities.
  • The delay in Fed easing cycles particularly hurts rate-sensitive Indian IT firms that derive over 60% of their revenue from the US market.
  • Elevated US yields are neutralizing positive domestic earnings surprises, forcing Indian investors to focus on stocks with intrinsic drivers rather than macro tailwinds.
  • USD strength resulting from the jobs data could increase the cost of dollar-priced commodities, potentially impacting the margins of Indian importers.
Earnings-Led Rotation into Banks and Industrial PSUs
  • HAL recorded a 30% profit jump and a ₹35 dividend, demonstrating strong cash-flow generation despite market concerns over aircraft delivery timelines.
  • Muthoot Finance reported a 95% surge in net profit, driven by high gold prices and a 48% growth in Assets Under Management for collateralized credit.
  • Kirloskar Oil Engines’ 56% profit jump signals a robust capital expenditure cycle currently fueling the Indian industrial and infrastructure machinery sectors.
  • Retail investor concentration in stocks like BHEL, with over 17 lakh small shareholders, creates a high-sensitivity environment prone to sharp volatility on earnings news.
  • Mahindra & Mahindra remains a prime beneficiary of both urban discretionary spending and rural cyclical recovery, supported by strong SUV market share gains.
