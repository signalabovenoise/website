Top News Developments
RBI Unleashes Rate Cut and Massive Liquidity Infusion to Stabilize Markets
  • The 25 basis point repo rate cut and durable liquidity operations signal a proactive easing stance by the RBI, aimed at stimulating economic activity and reducing funding costs for banks, which should translate to cheaper loans for consumers and businesses.
  • The ₹2 lakh crore OMO purchases and large variable-rate repo auctions are specifically designed to alleviate the current liquidity crunch caused by government cash balances, ensuring stable short-term interest rates and smoother corporate funding.
  • The USD 10 billion USD/INR buy-sell swap not only bolsters India's foreign exchange reserves but also strategically manages currency volatility, reducing the rupee's susceptibility to global fluctuations and protecting FPI confidence.
  • Sectors like banking, NBFCs, real estate, and autos are expected to benefit significantly from lower borrowing costs and improved credit availability, potentially driving their stock performance in the coming quarters.
  • For retail investors, this means a potentially more favorable environment for home and auto loans, while bond investors should anticipate downward pressure on G-sec yields and a re-evaluation of fixed-income portfolios.
FIIs Continue Selling Spree as DIIs Prop Up Indian Equities Amid Global Divergence
  • Foreign Institutional Investors have consistently been net sellers in 2025, offloading over ₹155,000 crore, driven by a weak rupee, high domestic valuations, and global rate divergences where India is easing while some advanced economies remain tighter.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors are acting as a strong counterweight, consistently buying to the tune of ₹3,812 crore on December 23rd, significantly cushioning the market against FII outflows and reflecting robust local investor confidence.
  • This dynamic suggests that while global factors can create headwinds, domestic liquidity and retail participation channeled through DIIs are providing crucial stability, preventing steeper market corrections.
  • Retail investors should recognize the strength of domestic flows as a market stabilizer but remain aware that persistent FII outflows could still cap upside potential, especially in large-cap stocks favored by foreign funds.
  • The sustained DII buying underscores a growing trend of financialization within Indian households, shifting savings into equities and offering a structural demand driver for the market, particularly for mid- and large-cap segments favored by analysts for 2026.
Global Commodity Rally Boosts Metals, While Crude Struggles and Gold Shines
  • Precious metals like gold, silver, and platinum hit record highs, fueled by expectations of US rate cuts, geopolitical tensions, and strong demand, with silver surging over 140% in 2025, signaling investor flight to safe havens and inflation hedges.
  • Copper prices soared above $12,000/ton, on track for a 40% annual gain, significantly boosting related stocks like Hindustan Copper to all-time highs, reflecting robust industrial demand and supply constraints.
  • In stark contrast, WTI Crude oil is heading for its worst annual performance since 2020, down 19% in 2025 due to global oversupply, which translates to potential cost relief for Indian industries reliant on crude imports.
  • For Indian retail investors, this divergent commodity performance means an opportunity in metal-producing companies and a potential reduction in input costs for sectors like manufacturing and transportation, but also a continued inflationary pressure from precious metals.
  • The bullish outlook for metals and the government's infrastructure push further reinforce the positive sentiment for related sectors, while the weakness in crude provides a macroeconomic tailwind for India's balance of payments.
India Inc. Shows Earnings Turnaround Amidst Key Sectoral and Corporate Developments
  • India Inc. recorded its first aggregate earnings upgrade in five quarters, signaling a turnaround in the corporate profit cycle driven by mid- and large-cap firms, which is a strong fundamental indicator for sustained market optimism in 2026.
  • The IT sector is regaining investor interest due to increasing AI revenue visibility and improved US demand, despite H-1B visa challenges, positioning key players like HCL Technologies (partnering with Microsoft for AI) for future growth.
  • The Indian REIT market surged past Hong Kong to ₹2.3 lakh crore, reflecting robust demand for commercial, housing, and hospitality spaces, indicating strong underlying real estate fundamentals and offering diversified investment avenues.
  • Railway stocks, like RVNL and Jupiter Wagons, are gaining focus ahead of Budget 2026 expectations, driven by significant government infrastructure contract awards, signaling sustained public sector capital expenditure.
  • Specific corporate actions, such as Vikran Engineering securing a massive solar project order equivalent to 95% of its market cap, and Muthoot Finance reporting a nearly 90% profit increase, highlight strong individual company performance and potential for significant shareholder value creation.

Policy & Regulations

  • The Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Committee cut the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.25%, maintaining a neutral stance while simultaneously arranging large durable liquidity injections via OMOs and FX buy-sell swaps, a move expected to reduce funding costs for banks and NBFCs, easing systemic liquidity strains and improving lending rates.
  • To explicitly address a liquidity shortfall driven by government cash balances and advance tax outflows, the RBI scheduled a substantial Open Market Operations purchase program aggregating ₹2 lakh crore and a ₹1.25 lakh crore variable-rate repo, alongside a USD 10 billion USD/INR buy-sell swap to bolster FX buffers.
  • On the regulatory front, SEBI’s enforcement-driven suspension of Jane Street Group's trading activities severely curtailed derivatives activity, with total option-premium turnover falling significantly, underscoring how swiftly targeted regulatory actions can impact market liquidity and derivatives pricing.
  • Furthermore, existing SEBI frameworks continue to restrict Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) to short-sell only stocks with less than 2% foreign shareholding and those not flagged on the RBI’s caution or ban list, which significantly limits short-selling exposure and shapes market dynamics in specific sectors.
  • Indian states are collectively set to borrow a record amount, a development that could impact bond markets by potentially keeping interest rates elevated, thereby affecting private borrowers and the broader cost of capital despite the RBI's easing measures.
