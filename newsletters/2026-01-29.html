Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • Indian equity markets opened on a muted note on January 29, 2026, with the Sensex and Nifty 50 trading near flat after two consecutive days of gains, as investor focus shifted to the Union Budget scheduled for February 1, 2026.
  • The Sensex recovered from an intraday low of 81,707.94 (down 637 points or 0.8%) to close above 81,780, while the Nifty 50 fell below the 25,200 mark before recovering to trade around 25,400 levels amid volatility.
  • Broad-based buying was supported by optimism around the India-European Union Free Trade Agreement and the Economic Survey 2026's positive GDP growth projections, which boosted investor sentiment.
  • Volatility remained elevated ahead of the Budget, with analysts expecting the Nifty to consolidate in the range of 24,900-25,550 levels; key support was identified at 25,200/82,000, with potential upside toward 25,500/82,800.
  • Foreign portfolio investors were net buyers of Rs 480 crore, while domestic institutional investors extended their buying streak for the 105th consecutive day with net purchases of Rs 3,360.59 crore, providing underlying support to equities.

Sector Highlights

Metal +3.07%

The metals sector surged 3.07%, driven by strong momentum in copper stocks. Hindustan Copper led the rally with an 18-19% surge, reaching an all-time high, benefiting from sector-specific tailwinds and positive investor sentiment. The metal index gained for the third consecutive day, reflecting sustained demand and favorable commodity price movements.

Energy +1.87%

The energy sector advanced 1.87%, supported by rising crude oil prices with Brent Crude trading near $69. Higher global oil prices provided a tailwind for energy stocks, though the search results do not identify specific energy sector gainers or company-level drivers beyond the broader commodity price environment.

Commodities +1.58%

The commodities sector gained 1.58%, riding on strength in precious metals, particularly gold and silver, which continued their rally. This performance aligns with the broader metals sector strength and reflects continued investor interest in commodity-linked assets amid global market dynamics.

Pharma -0.81%

The pharma sector declined 0.81% despite mixed earnings results. While Gland Pharma surged 9% to a two-month high on Q3 results, this was offset by declines in SBI Life shares, which fell 3% following earnings announcements. Sector weakness reflects profit-taking and selective sensitivity to individual company earnings performance.

FMCG -0.91%

The FMCG sector underperformed with a 0.91% decline, as the consumer durables segment lagged market performance. ITC shares also fell marginally amid broader consumer sector weakness, indicating cautious investor sentiment toward discretionary consumption despite mixed company-specific drivers.

Top News Developments
Economic Survey 2026 Boosts Market Confidence and Policy Outlook
  • The release of the Economic Survey 2026 triggered a sharp market recovery, with the BSE Sensex surging over 900 points from intraday lows, indicating renewed confidence in government policy and growth projections.
  • This pre-budget assessment provided crucial forward guidance, suggesting a positive policy direction for the fiscal year and potentially reducing near-term correction risks for equity valuations by signaling macroeconomic stability.
  • The strong bullish reaction implies significant support for infrastructure-heavy sectors and PSU stocks, which are highly dependent on government-led capital expenditure and policy continuity.
  • The Survey highlighted India's strong growth momentum amidst fragile global economic vulnerabilities, reassuring investors about the country's economic resilience and its favorable position internationally.
  • However, the Chief Economic Adviser's warning about stretched asset valuations and concentration risks suggests that retail investors should remain selective and cautious even amidst overall market positivity.
DII Buying Streak Continues as FIIs Reverse Trend, Bolstering Market Liquidity
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) extended an extraordinary 105-day consecutive buying streak, with net purchases of ₹3,360.59 crore, signifying sustained domestic confidence likely fueled by robust SIP inflows and mutual fund accumulation.
  • Crucially, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers after months of selling pressure, purchasing equities worth ₹480.26 crore, potentially marking a significant turning point in foreign sentiment towards Indian equities.
  • This powerful dual institutional support provides a strong liquidity floor, significantly reduces market vulnerability to external shocks, and could trigger a broader rally across all equity segments, from large to small caps.
  • The combined institutional buying, particularly the FII reversal, signals renewed global confidence in India's growth trajectory relative to other emerging markets and could lead to sustained equity valuations over the medium term.
  • This trend disproportionately benefits mid and small-cap segments often favored by DIIs, while FII re-entry supports quality large-cap names and could lead to improved stability and appreciation for the Indian rupee.
RVNL Secures Major Railway Project, Signals Infrastructure Capex Momentum
  • Rail Vikas Nigam Limited (RVNL) emerged as the lowest bidder for a significant ₹1,201 crore railway project from Northern Railway, substantially bolstering its order book and providing strong revenue pipeline visibility.
  • This large contract win unequivocally signals sustained government capital expenditure momentum in the vital railway sector, which is a key priority under India's ambitious National Infrastructure Pipeline.
  • The project win further validates the government's steadfast commitment to transportation modernization and demonstrates continued robust demand for PSU-linked infrastructure contractors, ensuring long-term project visibility.
  • Beyond RVNL, such large-scale announcements positively impact the broader Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) and infrastructure supply chain, including companies in steel (like Tata Steel), cement, and construction equipment.
  • This bullish development strongly supports RVNL's earnings growth forecasts for FY2026-27 and could lead to a re-rating of infrastructure PSUs if the accelerating order book growth continues its current trajectory.
Global Copper Demand Surge Elevates Prices, Benefits Indian Miners
  • Global copper demand has reached new highs, driven significantly by the proliferation of AI data centers and green energy initiatives, which are straining supplies and pushing prices to record levels worldwide.
  • This robust demand trend disproportionately benefits Indian copper miners like Hindustan Copper, whose shares surged to a 52-week high, further supported by the company securing new mining blocks to expand capacity.
  • Elevated copper prices are expected to persist, reflecting ongoing geopolitical tensions and broader global uncertainty, which directly impacts India's trade balance by increasing import costs for the vital metal.
  • The tight supply-demand dynamics in the global metals market underscore the critical importance of increasing domestic production and may lead to increased capital expenditure in India's mining sector.
  • Retail investors should closely monitor companies in the metals sector, as strong and sustained commodity prices translate directly into improved profitability and potentially significant stock performance for key industry players.
India-EU FTA and Rupee Volatility: Mixed Outlook for Indian Exports
  • The India-EU Free Trade Agreement is expected to significantly benefit Indian textile and pharmaceutical sectors through tariff reductions, offering new export opportunities and enhanced market access in Europe.
  • However, the FTA also presents notable challenges for sectors like capital goods and alcobev due to increased competition and the removal of existing trade barriers, requiring affected firms to adapt their strategic approaches.
  • The Indian rupee recently hit a record low, pressured by strong dollar demand and corporate hedging activities, despite the Economic Survey's assessment that the currency is currently undervalued.
  • This currency volatility could impact the cost of imports for Indian businesses and influence the profitability of companies with significant foreign exchange exposure, adding a layer of risk for investors.
  • A strategic shift in India's export landscape, moving away from the US market towards regions like the Middle East and Europe, as noted by the Economic Survey, is influencing the performance of traditional export-dependent sectors such as gems and jewellery, necessitating recalibration.

Policy & Regulations

  • The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has signaled heightened regulatory scrutiny on short-selling activities, warning that any fund or analyst organization engaging in shorting Indian equities could face close examination. This move aims to ensure market integrity and transparency, potentially impacting speculative trading strategies and encouraging a focus on long-term investment principles.
  • The Economic Survey 2026 proposes a new Securities Markets Code, indicating a comprehensive effort to reshape regulatory governance and coordinate reforms to deepen the corporate bond market. Such reforms are crucial for improving capital allocation efficiency and lowering credit costs for Indian businesses, which could attract more institutional investment into debt markets and support broader economic expansion.
  • The India-EU Free Trade Agreement (FTA) is expected to significantly benefit Indian textiles and pharmaceuticals through tariff reductions, potentially boosting their export competitiveness and market access in Europe.
