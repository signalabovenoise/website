Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • On 26 November 2025, the Indian stock market exhibited volatility with the Sensex closing around 84,587, down approximately 314 points (0.37%), and the Nifty 50 ending near 25,885, down 75 points (0.29%) due to late-session selling pressure, especially in banking and IT sectors.
  • Retail investor sentiment appeared cautious amid profit-booking ahead of the monthly F&O expiry, with broader markets like midcap and smallcap indices witnessing mild gains, outperforming the benchmark indices.
  • Metal, pharma, PSU banks, and realty sectors showed resilience with gains between 0.5-1%, while consumer durables, IT, media, and oil & gas sectors declined by around 0.5% each.
  • The market was affected by brokerage downgrades impacting Oil Marketing Companies and heavyweights such as Infosys, HDFC Bank, and Kotak Bank dragged the market lower.
  • Despite intraday volatility and a mostly negative close, some rebound was visible in later sessions with the Nifty testing levels near 26,000, indicating a cautious but not overly bearish overall trend.

Sector Highlights

Metal +2.06%

The Metal sector rose 2.06% driven by strong gains in key stocks such as National Aluminium and SAIL, supported by robust intraday momentum and positive structural outlook amid expectations of increased demand and potential commodity price stability. The rally was also aided by favorable global cues and domestic buying interest in metal stocks.

Commodities +1.79%

Commodities gained 1.79%, boosted by rising global commodity prices, including gold edging up on hopes of a Federal Reserve rate cut in December, which buoyed risk appetite. Improved demand expectations and positive sentiment around commodity-linked stocks contributed to the sector's uptick today.

Energy +1.74%

Energy stocks advanced 1.74% amid supportive crude oil price trends and anticipation of stable demand in domestic and international markets. The global rally and expectations of an accommodative monetary policy in the US lent further strength to energy names in India.

PSU Bank +0.77%

PSU Bank sector’s 0.77% gain was led by strong stock-specific rallies such as Bank of India and Indian Overseas Bank, driven by positive corporate developments including a tax refund order for Indian Overseas Bank and attractive valuation amidst expectations of RBI’s rate cuts in December, enhancing lending prospects and sector momentum.

FMCG +0.71%

FMCG sector’s 0.71% rise reflected steady investor interest supported by defensive quality amid market volatility, with selective buying in large-cap FMCG stocks. The sector benefited from domestic consumption resilience and favorable outlook amidst global uncertainties.

Top News Developments
Market Hits Record Highs on Global Tailwinds
  • Benchmark indices surged over 1.2%, driven primarily by strong global cues, particularly rising expectations of a US Federal Reserve rate cut in December which boosted risk appetite worldwide.
  • The rally was broad-based, with mid-cap and small-cap indices participating strongly, indicating widespread investor confidence beyond just large-cap stocks.
  • A significant fall in Brent crude oil prices to around $61/barrel provided a major boost, as it lowers India's import bill, eases inflation concerns, and improves margins for key sectors.
  • Strong and consistent buying from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided a powerful cushion, ensuring the market's upward momentum was sustained despite historical FII outflows.
PSU Banks and Metals Lead Sectoral Charge
  • PSU Banks were the standout performers, with the Nifty Bank index scaling a fresh all-time high, led by stocks like State Bank of India.
  • This rally in PSU banks is fueled by their sensitivity to interest rates; investors are positioning for lower funding costs and improved margins in an anticipated rate-cut cycle.
  • Metal stocks also witnessed a sharp surge, directly benefiting from the proposed Draft Electricity (Amendment) Bill which promises to lower energy costs, a major operational expense for the sector.
  • This sectoral rotation into undervalued PSU banks and cyclicals like metals suggests a potential shift in market leadership away from previously over-bought sectors.
Crude Oil's Slump Offers Economic Relief
  • Brent crude prices fell to around $61 per barrel, with long-term forecasts from JP Morgan suggesting a potential drop to $30 by FY27.
  • For the Indian economy, which is a major oil importer, this is a significant positive development that helps control both the trade deficit and domestic inflation.
  • Sectors that use crude derivatives as raw materials, such as paints, tyres, and chemicals, are direct beneficiaries and could see substantial margin expansion.
  • Aviation companies also stand to gain significantly from lower fuel costs, which could improve their profitability and make air travel more affordable.
IPO Frenzy Continues Amid Valuation Concerns
  • India's primary market is gearing up for a record-breaking December with an expected IPO pipeline of up to ₹40,000 crore from companies like Meesho and Fractal Analytics.
  • Strong investor appetite was confirmed by the premium listing of Excelsoft Technologies, signaling robust demand for new issues with sound fundamentals.
  • However, some analysts are raising red flags about the steep valuations of certain venture-funded tech companies coming to market.
  • This heavy supply of new equity, while reflecting a vibrant market, could also absorb liquidity and create underlying cautiousness, even as benchmark indices climb higher.

Emerging Opportunities

The metal sector is emerging as a key investment opportunity due to recent strength and gains around 4%, driven by expectations of sustained demand and price stability.
PSU banks are showing promise, trading at record highs with supportive government policies and improving asset quality, making them attractive for medium- to long-term gains.
Pharmaceuticals and healthcare sectors present growth potential thanks to ongoing policy support and robust domestic demand amidst global health awareness.
Technology and IT sectors are candidates for selective picking given current sell-offs, potentially offering contrarian opportunities as companies innovate and export tech services.
Renewable energy and green infrastructure companies are gaining attention due to new policy developments and heightened focus on sustainability and environmental priorities.

Policy & Regulations

  • SEBI has proposed to simplify the procedure for investors to obtain duplicate physical share certificates by increasing the documentation limit to ₹10 lakh, a move that will reduce compliance burdens and improve ease of transaction for retail investors holding physical shares.
  • The government's Draft Electricity (Amendment) Bill, 2025, proposes to eliminate cross-subsidies, which could significantly lower power costs for industrial users.
  • This electricity reform is a major positive catalyst for energy-intensive sectors such as steel, cement, and aluminum, potentially leading to improved margins and increased competitiveness for companies in these industries.
