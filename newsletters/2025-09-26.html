Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • On 26 September 2025, the Indian stock market experienced a notable downturn with the BSE Sensex closing 0.68% lower at 81,159.68 and the NSE Nifty 50 down 0.66%, ending at 24,890.85 amid negative investor sentiment.
  • Retail investor sentiment was cautious and subdued, affected by ongoing uncertainty and a continuation of foreign institutional investors (FIIs) net selling for a fourth consecutive session, although domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided support through buying.
  • Market volatility was high as investors reacted to global economic uncertainties and domestic factors, with selling pressure seen especially in the pharmaceutical and IT sectors.
  • The Indian rupee remained under pressure, nearing record lows against the US dollar at around 88.79, reflecting external vulnerabilities that weighed on overall market confidence.
  • Overall, the market environment was characterized by risk aversion and confusion, influenced by geopolitical and tariff-related developments impacting key sectors, leading to a cautious trading day.

Realty -1.00%

Auto -1.02%

Auto sector declined 1.02%, influenced by continued selling pressure due to tariff concerns on heavy-duty trucks by the US (25% tariff announced), dampening export outlook and cost structures for manufacturers.

Pharma -2.14%

Pharma sector declined sharply by 2.14% triggered by President Trump's announcement of a 100% tariff on branded or patented pharmaceutical products effective October 1, 2025, unless companies have begun building manufacturing plants in the US. This escalates fears of reduced exports and margin pressure.

IT -2.45%

IT sector was down 2.45% due to renewed concerns over US trade policies impacting Indian exporters, including tariff risks and geopolitical uncertainties, resulting in broad foreign investment outflows and pessimism in the sector.

Top News Developments
US Tariff Blow to Indian Pharma Exports
  • The US announced a 100% tariff on Indian branded pharmaceutical exports, set to commence October 1, severely impacting India’s global market competitiveness.
  • Top pharma companies, including Sun Pharma and Cipla, face significant earnings risks, with stock prices already under pressure.
  • This development may lead to disruptions in pharma sector supply chains, prompting exploration of alternate export markets or price renegotiations.
FII Sell-Off Continues, Rupee Near Record Lows
  • FIIs pulled ₹5,098 crore out of Indian markets recently, marking heavy foreign outflows amid global geopolitical uncertainties.
  • The Indian rupee weakened to 88.79 per USD, increasing import costs and inflationary pressures, especially for sectors reliant on dollar-denominated inputs.
  • Banking, FMCG, and IT sectors are seeing mixed impacts, with asset pricing and earnings vulnerability intensifying due to forex risks.
Defence Stocks Under Pressure Amid Market Weakness
  • Defence stocks like Hindustan Aeronautics lost ground amidst concerns over lower government budget allocations for defence procurement.
  • Market-wide sell-offs fueled negative sentiment in heavy industries, undermining confidence in stocks tied to public spending like Tata Steel.
  • Investors are advised to monitor government policy responses closely, as global uncertainties continue to weigh on defence sector valuations.

Emerging Opportunities

Despite current headwinds, opportunities persist in the renewable energy sector, bolstered by recently announced supportive government policies aimed at scaling clean energy capacity and infrastructure.
The technology sector, especially companies focused on domestic digital infrastructure and AI-driven services, remains promising for medium- to long-term growth due to increasing digital adoption in India.
Financial services linked to rural and digital payments show potential growth driven by ongoing financial inclusion initiatives and advancements in fintech platforms.
Pharmaceuticals, though currently pressured, may present contrarian investment opportunities based on India's strong generic drug manufacturing base and evolving export markets beyond the US.
Infrastructure development tied to upcoming government-backed urban development and transport projects could offer growth avenues in construction materials, engineering, and related services sectors over the coming years.
