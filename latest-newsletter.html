Sector Highlights

IT +3.34%

IT stocks surged up to 6% driven by strong Q3 results and upgraded FY26 revenue guidance from Infosys, the sector leader. Top gainers included Infosys (+5.42%), Tech Mahindra (+4.28%), and Wipro (+3.21%), with the Nifty IT index hitting a three-week high. The positive earnings and forward guidance from a major industry player boosted investor confidence in the sector's growth prospects.

PSU Bank +1.16%

PSU bank stocks gained 2.1%, supported by healthy Q3 FY26 business updates from select lenders. The sector benefited from positive quarterly earnings announcements that demonstrated strong operational performance and financial health.

Bank +0.86%

The Nifty Bank index rose 0.86%, reclaiming the 60,000 level after 4 days ahead of Q3 results from major banks like ICICI and HDFC Bank. While the broader banking sector showed modest gains, performance was uneven, with some large-cap banks like ICICI Bank and Kotak Bank declining slightly despite sector strength.

CPSE -0.88%

CPSE stocks declined 0.88%. Despite some strength in specific segments—oil and gas stocks (HPCL, BPCL, IOC) jumped up to 4% on declining crude oil prices amid easing US-Iran tensions—the overall CPSE index fell, suggesting weakness in other state-owned enterprise segments outweighed gains in the energy subsector.

Pharma -1.28%

Pharma stocks declined 1.28%, with Sun Pharma posting the largest loss at -1.76% among Sensex constituents. The search results do not provide specific sector-level catalysts or news explaining the decline, indicating the weakness may reflect broader market concerns or rotation away from the sector.

Top News Developments
Persistent FII Selling Amid US Tariff Threats Continues to Weigh on Indian Markets
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, offloading ₹4,346 crore on January 16, extending a consistent trend of outflows since early 2025, largely driven by global risk aversion due to US tariff threats and stalled India-US trade negotiations.
  • This sustained FII selling pressure is causing liquidity drain and increased volatility in large-cap segments, challenging the broader Nifty's ability to maintain upward momentum and keeping it range-bound.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) acted as a crucial counterweight, with net purchases of ₹3,935 crore, predominantly supporting banking and PSU stocks and helping to stabilize the market.
  • The ongoing FII risk aversion caps upside potential and could lead to rupee depreciation, potentially influencing the Reserve Bank of India's stance on future rate cuts.
  • Retail investors should closely monitor upcoming developments like the Union Budget 2026 or clearer progress in trade talks, as these could act as catalysts to reverse FII sentiment and bring fresh inflows.
Escalating Geopolitical Tensions Fuel Global Risk-Off Sentiment, Bolstering Gold Demand
  • Heightened geopolitical tensions in regions like Venezuela, Greenland, and Iran are fueling global risk-off sentiment, contributing to the Indian market's cautious opening and amplifying oil price volatility.
  • As India is a net oil importer, rising crude prices due to supply risks pose a bearish outlook for oil importing sectors and can lead to increased inflation passed through to wholesale prices, which recently hit an 8-month high.
  • This global uncertainty has boosted safe-haven demand, leading to a rally in gold prices and creating a bullish outlook for gold and related assets like gold ETFs and jewellery stocks.
  • The elevated VIX index, a measure of market volatility, reflects investor anxiety and is curbing overall market liquidity, making cautious investment approaches advisable.
  • Investors may consider diversifying into defensive assets like gold or exploring opportunities in defence-related stocks, while being mindful of the potential negative impact on energy and infrastructure supply chains if tensions escalate further.
Infosys Q3 Earnings Anticipation Drives Gains Amid Mixed IT Sector Performance
  • Infosys experienced significant pre-market buzz and a 5.63% surge ahead of its Q3FY26 earnings report, contributing substantially to Nifty's gains and reflecting strong stock-specific optimism.
  • The company's upgraded FY26 guidance to 3-3.5% provides a positive signal, suggesting a better-than-expected outlook despite the broader IT sector grappling with a US slowdown and tariff concerns impacting client spending.
  • However, the overall Q3FY26 IT earnings season has been mixed, with Tech Mahindra reporting strong profit growth and deal wins, while Wipro's profit declined and Tata Technologies saw a sharp plunge due to new labor codes.
  • This divergence underscores the need for retail investors to focus on individual company fundamentals, deal wins, and guidance rather than broad sector movements, as performance varies greatly among IT majors.
  • Should the broader IT sector disappoint, there is a potential for sector rotation into more resilient areas like banking, emphasizing the dynamic nature of capital flows during earnings season.

Policy & Regulations

  • SEBI has introduced new regulations effective April 1, 2026, which will lower mutual fund expense ratio caps and implement a base expense ratio (BER) structure. This is expected to impact the profitability of asset management companies and make mutual fund investments more cost-effective for retail investors.
  • SEBI proposed easing foreign investor operations by allowing net trade settlements and streamlining Know Your Customer (KYC) updates via KRAs. This move is aimed at lowering funding costs and improving operational efficiency for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs).
  • The Union Ministry of Power clarified that the recently announced 'green-energy credit' scheme would be deferred pending a fiscal-policy review, removing a key near-term catalyst for renewable-energy developers and influencing investor sentiment in the sector.
  • A recent verdict stressing national sovereignty in international taxation is causing concern among tax experts, who warn it may encourage authorities to reopen existing Private Equity (PE), Venture Capital (VC), and FPI deals, creating potential uncertainty for foreign investors.
  • The Reserve Bank of India's monthly monetary-policy review highlighted a rise in non-performing assets within the micro-finance segment, signaling potential headwinds for banks with significant exposure to this lending category.
