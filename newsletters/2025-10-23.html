Market Summary

Daily Overview

  • The BSE Sensex closed higher by around 130 points, up approximately 0.1%, while the NSE Nifty ended with a 23-point gain, around 0.1%, continuing their winning streak for the sixth consecutive session on 23 October 2025.

  • Retail investor sentiment was cautiously optimistic, supported by positive earnings expectations, robust festive season sales projections, and recent policy measures including GST rate cuts enhancing corporate earnings outlook.
  • Sectoral performance was mixed with strong buying in IT and media sectors while oil & gas and services sectors faced selling pressure; IT and banking stocks supported the market gains significantly.
  • The Indian rupee traded around 88.7 to the US dollar, reflecting stable currency conditions amidst market optimism and flows of foreign investment.
  • Overall, the market showed confidence backed by anticipated favorable India-US trade developments and fresh foreign investments, reinforcing a positive environment for equities on this day.

Top News Developments
RBI's Twin Boost: Growth Up, Inflation Down
  • The RBI's decision to keep the repo rate unchanged while upgrading the GDP growth forecast to 6.8% and lowering the inflation forecast to 2.6% for FY26 has created a highly favorable macroeconomic backdrop.
  • This 'goldilocks' scenario of high growth and low inflation directly benefits rate-sensitive sectors like banking, auto, and real estate by supporting credit demand and affordability.
  • Lower inflation boosts real consumer purchasing power, providing a strong tailwind for consumer discretionary and FMCG companies.
  • The stable policy environment reinforces investor confidence, supporting both equity valuations and foreign fund inflows into the market.
Trade Deal Buzz Powers Market Rally
  • Market sentiment is being primarily driven by strong expectations of a favorable US-India trade agreement, which has fueled the Nifty's sixth consecutive day of gains.
  • Sectors with high export exposure are seeing direct benefits, with textile stocks rallying significantly on hopes that US import duties could be slashed from 50% to around 15%.
  • This optimism has been a key factor in attracting Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) back to the Indian market, reversing previous outflows.
  • The prospect of a deal is also strengthening the Indian Rupee against the US Dollar, further improving the outlook for the Indian economy.
Infosys Buyback Ignites IT Sector
  • Infosys's announcement of a massive ₹18,000 crore share buyback acted as a major catalyst, causing its stock to surge and lifting the entire Nifty IT index.
  • The decision for promoters to not participate is a significant positive for retail investors, as it increases the probability of their shares being accepted in the buyback.
  • This corporate action is viewed as a strong signal from management that they believe the company's shares are undervalued, boosting overall investor confidence in the sector.
  • The move creates a positive ripple effect, drawing investor attention back to the IT sector, which had been underperforming, and benefiting peers like TCS and HCL Tech.

Emerging Opportunities

Information Technology sector continues to present strong growth potential fueled by global digital transformation trends and robust earnings, making IT companies like Infosys, HCL Tech, and TCS attractive medium- to long-term investments.
Banking and financial services sectors are emerging as promising areas with the Nifty Bank index reaching new highs, supported by strong credit growth and financial inclusion efforts.
Textile and consumer goods sectors show opportunistic value given festive sales momentum and specific stock rallies such as Vardhaman Textiles signaling potential contrarian plays.
Policy developments including easing GST rates and expected India-US trade deal tariff reductions could catalyze export-oriented sectors and manufacturing, creating investment avenues in these areas.
Pharma and healthcare sectors are gaining traction due to rising domestic demand and export potential, bolstered by innovation and capacity expansions, marking them as strategic bets for future growth.

Policy & Regulations

  • The Reserve Bank of India announced significant regulatory reforms for the banking sector, including the implementation of updated Basel III norms and Expected Credit Loss (ECL) models to enhance stability and global alignment.
  • RBI also established a formal monetary policy framework that prioritizes price stability with a clear inflation target, aiming to provide greater transparency and predictability for investors.
  • Separately, SEBI has proposed a standardized process for opening new mutual fund folios which will mandate KYC verification by a registration agency, aiming to streamline investor onboarding and strengthen compliance.
